ABOUT THE PROJECT

Project overview

Underworld is a 2D pixel-art action-adventure platformer and the first complete game my brother and I developed together under Nitrous Studio.

Players explore a decaying sci-fi world as a nano-enhanced swordsman, unlocking new abilities, discovering hidden areas and facing increasingly dangerous enemies.

Inspired by Metroidvania exploration and Souls-like combat, the game combines semi-linear progression, platforming, responsive melee combat and multi-phase boss encounters.

My contributions

Working collaboratively within a two-person team, I contributed to the design, implementation and refinement of the following systems:

  • Dynamic and context-sensitive camera system.
  • Combat, parrying, abilities and hit detection.
  • Finite State Machine boss architecture and health-based attack phases.
  • Boss mechanics, attacks and multi-phase encounter design.
  • Level design, encounter placement, tutorial flow and environmental presentation.
  • UI, ability progression, collectibles and merchant features.
  • Gameplay polish, debugging and pre/post-release fixes.

Gameplay & Technical Systems

A closer look at the gameplay systems, architectural decisions and design work behind Underworld.

01 / GAMEPLAY PROGRAMMING

Combat, Parry & Ability System

Underworld's combat system combines consecutive melee attacks, defensive parrying and unlockable abilities within a responsive combat loop.

Parry & Combat System

The parry uses a dedicated collision area, independent from normal attacks and abilities. It can intercept and destroy incoming projectiles. The base attack combo was also made loopable to improve consecutive attack flow.

Animation-driven Abilities

Abilities are implemented as separate prefabs, temporarily restricting normal player input through the isAbility state. Animation events synchronize hit detection with specific moments of each attack animation.

Multi-hit abilities can modify facingDirection during execution, repositioning their attack colliders accordingly.

Ability Progression & Persistence

Ability unlocks are persisted using PlayerPrefs, while their acquisition order is stored separately to reconstruct the correct HUD arrangement.

This ensures that unlocked abilities remain available following player death.

02 / CAMERA ARCHITECTURE

Dynamic Camera System

A contextual camera system developed using Cinemachine to adapt framing to the player's movement and surrounding environment.

Trigger-Based Framing

The CameraControlTrigger component can pan the active camera or transition between virtual cameras depending on the player's exit direction.

Configurable Camera Zones

Camera triggers expose configurable pan direction, distance, duration and camera references. Camera offsets are interpolated using coroutines and Vector3.Lerp for smooth transitions.

Movement-Responsive Camera

Camera framing adjusts to the player's movement direction. During rapid falls, Cinemachine's vertical damping is dynamically modified using Rigidbody2D.velocity.y.

This improves environmental visibility during platforming and vertical traversal.

03 / AI & ENCOUNTER DESIGN

Boss & Encounter Systems

We implemented boss behaviours through a stack-driven Finite State Machine using the State design pattern.

State-Pattern FSM

Boss behaviours implement Enter(), Update() and Exit(), separating distinct states such as chasing, melee attacks, teleportation, projectile attacks and death.

Stack-Based Attack Phases

Attack sequences are defined as List<IState> and executed through a Stack<IState>.

Different sequences are loaded when the boss reaches specific health thresholds, creating distinct combat phases.

Multi-Step Attacks

Complex attacks use internal step-based substates to coordinate animations, movement, timers, collision activation, lasers and projectiles throughout an attack sequence.

NARRATIVE DESIGN

Storytelling through Webcomics

Underworld uses comic-book sequences as narrative cutscenes throughout the game, including its introduction, merchant interactions and boss encounters. I also contributed to the writing of these sequences.
